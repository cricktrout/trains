Track Gauge

Track gauge or rail gauge is the distance between the inner sides of the heads of the two load bearing rails that make up a single railway line. In the United States, standard gauge (Stephenson) measures 56.5 inches (4 feet 8.5 inches) and narrow gauge (ng) measures 36.0 inches. (3 feet) Wider gauges are called broad gauge; smaller gauges, narrow gauge. Some references list U.S. narrow gauge measuring between 2 feet and 3 feet 6 inches. Sixty percent of the world's railways use Stephenson's 4 ft 8½ in standard gauge. Some stretches of track are dual gauge, with three or four rails, allowing trains of different gauges to share them. Realistically, using a standard name for a gauge is at best misleading and at worst inaccurate since individual railroad companies and various countries around the world use(d) a wide variety of gauges, some up to 7+ feet. For this reason, all gauges on this site will be listed as the actual measurement (If known) and not simply “standard” or “narrow.”

The U.S. Federal Railroad Administration specifies that the actual gauge of track that is rated for a maximum of 60 mph must be between 4 ft 8 in and 4 ft 9½ in.
