F. M. Whyte Wheel Classification Notation

The F. M. Whyte wheel classification notation is a system developed by Frederick Methvan Whyte to classify steam locomotives by wheel arrangement. Starting from the front of the locomotive, count the axles in each group of axles. Each group will be represented by the number of wheels in each group. (2 wheels x # of axles) Each group will be separated by a dash (-) The first group will be the wheels before the drivers. The second group will be the drivers and the third group will be the wheels after the drivers. If there are no axles in a non-driver group then that group will be indicated by a “0”.

Example: a locomotive with 2 leading non-drive axles followed by 2 drive axles and no trailing non-drive axles would be a 4-4-0.


An articulated locomotive is essentially two locomotives joined together that share a common boiler. This will be indicated with a plus (+) between the joined sections. If there are no intermediate unpowered axles then the “0” notation will be included.

Example: 4-6-2+2-6-4 or 4-6-0+0-6-4.


Sometimes locomotives can be joined together but will not be articulated and have no unpowered axles in between groups of powered groups such as the Mallet. This will be indicated by inserting another group in the middle.

Example: a locomotive with 2 leading non-drive axles followed by 4 drive axles, a second group of 4 drive axles and 2 trailing non-drive axles would be a 4-8-8-4.


Suffixes are used to further describe certain features of a locomotive. A tender locomotive is assumed. Some of the more common suffixes are:

TTank
STSaddle Tank
RTRack or Reversible Tank
WTWell Tank
CACompressed Air

Another practice is “Naming” common wheel arrangements. Many of the names were chosen based on the first locomotive to have a particular wheel arrangement. Some more recognized examples are listed below.

Some locomotives such as Shay, Heisler or Climax are geared locomotives and don’t fit into this classification. Therefore, on this site, they will simply be listed by manufacturer and number of trucks.
